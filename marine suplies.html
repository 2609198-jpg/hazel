The history of marine supplies

• Early Ports: Ancient ports in Lamu, Malindi, and Mombasa hosted Chinese armadas, Ottoman fleets, and Portuguese vessels trading spices, ivory, and marine cargo. • Traditional Craft: Local fishermen and coastal traders relied heavily on hand-carved wooden dhows and dugout canoes built from indigenous coastal timber. Colonial and Early Post-Independence Era (1890s – 1990s) • The Uganda Railway (1901): The completion of the railway from Mombasa to Lake Victoria (Port Florence) transformed interior-to-coast logistics and boosted regional shipping. • Institutional Foundations: The Kenya Marine and Fisheries Research Institute (KMFRI) was established in 1979 to manage aquatic resources and research. • Early Commercial Suppliers: Dedicated private marine equipment and commercial fishing supply companies began forming in the late 20th century to service commercial fisheries, tourism, and port operations around Mombasa.

Marine Communication Equipment

• VHF (Very High Frequency) Radios: Used for short-range ship-to-ship and ship-to-shore communication (typically within 20 to 30 nautical miles). They operate on the 156–174 MHz band, with Channel 16 reserved for international distress and safety, and Channel 70 for Digital Selective Calling (DSC). • MF/HF (Medium Frequency / High Frequency) Radios: Used for long-distance and ocean-crossing communication. They utilize Single Sideband (SSB) technology to connect with distant shore stations or other vessels hundreds or thousands of miles away. • Satellite Communication Systems (Inmarsat / Iridium): Provide global voice, data, internet, and distress messaging independent of terrestrial mast ranges. • AIS (Automatic Identification System): Broadcasts and receives real-time vessel data—including identity, position, speed, and heading—via VHF channels to aid in collision avoidance. • EPIRB (Emergency Position-Indicating Radio Beacon): A satellite-linked distress beacon (406 MHz) that activates during a sinking or critical emergency to transmit a vessel's position to rescue authorities. • SART (Search and Rescue Transponder): A 9 GHz radar transponder that helps rescue teams pinpoint a distressed vessel or survival craft by displaying a pattern of dots on their radar screen. • NAVTEX: An automated receiver that prints or displays coastal Maritime Safety Information (MSI), including navigational warnings, meteorological forecasts, and distress alerts on 518 kHz.
